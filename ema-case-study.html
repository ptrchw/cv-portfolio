Case Study 01 · Public Sector · Digital Transformation

Architecting a Modern UX for Four Legacy National Energy Platforms

As Experience Design Lead within NEC Asia Pacific's team, I led the experience design workstream for a multi-year digital transformation programme with Singapore's Energy Market Authority (EMA) — unifying four distinct government digital platforms under one consistent, mobile-first brand and design system: the EMA Corporate Website, the Energy Industry Scholarship (EIS) portal, the e-Licensing Information Services (ELISE) portal, and the Singapore International Energy Week (SIEW) website.

RoleExperience Design Lead
MethodUser research × service design × design systems
Scope4 government digital platforms · one shared brand system
StatusDelivered · Oct 2022 – May 2025

The Context

A legacy, non-responsive system built on manual processes — overdue for a digital transformation.

Singapore's Energy Market Authority runs several distinct digital services for very different audiences — households and businesses buying electricity, licensed workers and installers, scholarship applicants, and an international industry conference. Each had grown up on its own, without a shared design language.

What the digital estate looked like

  • Legacy CMS and infrastructure, with no user-friendly way to manage content
  • Outdated, desktop-first interfaces across most touchpoints
  • Four platforms, four different visual identities
  • Scattered information architecture that was hard to navigate
  • Limited analytics and content tracking

What the transformation asked for

  • How might we design one mobile-first, SGDS-aligned system flexible enough for four very different platforms?
  • How might we let research and service design reshape the structure, not just the visuals?
  • How might we validate every touchpoint with real users before it ships?
  • How might we streamline the CMS so that non-technical staff are empowered to manage content effortlessly?

This wasn't a single website redesign; it was building a brand and experience system disciplined enough to hold together across four different platforms and four different user groups, at government scale.

The Four Platforms

One brand system, four very different jobs-to-be-done.

Each platform serves a distinct audience — but all four now share the same visual language, navigation patterns and mobile-first foundation.

EMA Corporate Website

ema.gov.sg

EMA's primary public-facing site: energy sector news, consumer information, regulations & licences, and Singapore's official energy statistics publication.

EIS Portal

Energy Industry Scholarship

A dedicated scholarship portal connecting students and sponsoring organisations to talent development in Singapore's energy sector.

ELISE Portal

e-Licensing Information Services

A one-stop licensing portal for licensed workers and installers — covering everything from solar PV to energy storage systems.

SIEW Website

Singapore International Energy Week

The flagship site for Singapore's premier international energy event — registration, programme and partner information.

How It Came Together

From research to a validated, shared design system.

Every decision was grounded in extensive user research and real data, not assumption. Workflows were co-created with stakeholders through a series of in-depth workshops, then validated end-to-end through rounds of usability testing before anything shipped.

01

Discovery & research

Primary and secondary research — user interviews, surveys, website audits and analytics review — across all four platforms to understand where each was falling short.

02

Framing & structure

Personas, user journey mapping and service blueprints turned research into a clear picture of how citizens, licensees, scholars and industry partners actually move through EMA's digital services — followed by a unified information architecture and mega-menu structure.

03

Design system & prototyping

A single, mobile-first design language — aligned to the Singapore Government Design System (SGDS) — applied consistently from wireframes through to high-fidelity UI and interactive prototypes.

04

Validation & delivery

Structured usability testing validated the designs with real users before build, refined through ongoing stakeholder workshops across the programme.
