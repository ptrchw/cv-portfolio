Case Study 04 · Vibe-Coded Product

Two players. Two themes. One thoughtful user-centred system.

NumLah is a responsible-play utility for checking Singapore 4D & TOTO numbers — deliberately designed as two switchable custom themes, "Chit Chat" and "Teh Tarik," built for two very different players over one shared product logic. I used Claude to take it from brainstorming to persona research through two fully working theme prototypes — each with its own design language and voice — prototyped, tested and QA'd end-to-end through AI-assisted development.

RoleSolo — research, design & build
MethodVibe-coded with Claude
StackHTML / CSS / JS — no framework
StatusProof of concept, not shipped

The Thesis

Everyone will build one app. Few will design for who's actually holding the phone.

Most apps flatten every player into one interface. But the person who plays the same birthday numbers for thirty years is nothing like the one chasing a lucky feeling on a whim. A single UI compromises both. So I built two custom themes for two identified user personas.

What most apps optimise for

  • One layout
  • One tone
  • One icon set
  • An "average" user who doesn't exist
  • Ship once, never revisit

What this project asked instead

  • How should the interface change when the player is a retiree checking a ritual, versus a casual dreamer chasing a feeling?
  • Can two design languages share one product logic without becoming two separate apps to maintain?
  • How do you let someone choose the version built for them, the moment they open the app?

These aren't merely technical questions; they are matters of design and system architecture. This is exactly where user-centred design, thoughtful interaction, and structural judgment make the difference between a superficial demo and a robust, sustainable product.

The Framing

Two personas became two design languages.

Rather than averaging both players into one compromise, each got a design built from their real needs — as a switchable theme within one product, not a separate app.

About

Applied Design Thinking

Persona drives every decision — not taste.

Every visible difference between the two themes traces back to a documented user need. Same product logic underneath; opposite expression on top.

Ethical Guardrails

Designing responsibly, by default.

This is a gambling-adjacent product. Responsible-play principles were woven into the UX from the first screen — not bolted on at the end.

01

Never predictive

Every analysis screen is descriptive only. The copy stays careful: every draw is independent, and past results can't tell you the next one.

02

Independence stated

The app repeatedly reinforces that draws are independent random events, countering the gambler's fallacy in context, not just in a footnote.

03

Calm, non-bold disclaimers

Disclaimer copy renders in regular weight in both themes — present and readable, never a hype-inducing shout.

04

Help is one tap away

The NCPG helpline (1800-6-668-668) is surfaced directly in-app — the tone stays supportive, never pushy toward more play.

Bringing It To Life

Built end-to-end with Claude, one decision at a time.

What began as a single proof-of-concept evolved into two fully developed, themed prototypes built on a unified product logic. Every phase detailed below reflects intentional design choices and meticulous, step-by-step iteration.

01

Two personas, decided upfront

Sarah Lim (casual dreamer) and Uncle Cheng (routine regular) were kept as two separate people with different needs from day one — never merged into one persona with a common personality.

02

One POC, two theme forks

Started as a single React proof of concept, then forked into two themes — Chit Chat (playful, feature-rich) and Teh Tarik (calm, ledger-style) — each with its own design language, copy voice and icon system, over the same underlying product logic.

03

A Reality Check, framed honestly

A backtesting screen against real Singapore Pools historical odds — built to explore patterns transparently, while stating plainly, everywhere, that every draw is still an independent, unpredictable event.

04

Bilingual, built one way, ported the other

Full English / 中文 support — built first inside Teh Tarik, then the whole pattern (STRINGS engine, language rocker) was carried over and re-fitted into Chit Chat.

05

A full regression pass, not a guess

Every interaction — keypads, the TOTO 7-number cap, delete-confirm flows, menus, the Shake Me Lah drag gesture — verified directly in a real browser, not assumed from reading the code.

The Flow

One flow, tuned two ways.

Both themes share the same underlying journey. Every screen exists in both — the skin and the motion are what differ, and Chit Chat layers a few extra screens on top for its more exploratory persona.

Splash My Numbers Add Entry — 4D / TOTO Processing Results & Analysis

+ Menu drawer → Notifications · Preferences · About, reachable from the ledger anytime. Chit Chat additionally offers a Reality Check backtester, History and Favourites for its more exploratory persona.

Shared underneath

Draw schedules, entry storage, 4D/TOTO descriptive analysis, notifications and core navigation — the same logic in both themes.

Divergent on top

Layout density, type scale, colour, icon system, copy voice and motion character all change per theme — and Chit Chat's larger feature set for its more exploratory persona.

Interaction & Motion

Motion with a personality.

Animation isn't decoration here — it expresses each persona. Every transition was hand-tuned to feel right for the person it serves.

Chit Chat — playful

Shake Me Lah A draggable edge tab — tap to expand it, tap again to trigger a shake that fills in a random number.
shakeWiggle The tab itself wiggles side to side as the shake fires — a quick, playful confirmation that the gesture registered.
circleRipple On the processing screen, two rings pulse outward from the ticket logo in a continuous loop — a livelier loading state than a plain spinner.
ticketSpin2D / splashRise The same processing ticket spins with energy; splash elements rise in with a spring-like ease.

Teh Tarik — composed

spinTicket A single calm ticket-logo spin during processing — patience, not spectacle.
overlay slide · 0.25s Screens slide in from the right like turning a passbook page.
snackbar rise A gentle rise-and-settle confirms actions like "Number deleted", with an Undo close at hand.
active-press feedback Buttons inset and shift slightly on press — physical, ledger-stamp tactility.

Architecture For Scale

Designed to become one app.

The two files stay separate today — but the intended production shape is a single app: one logic core, swappable theme layers, chosen at first launch.

Chit Chat theme

tokens · components · icons · motion

Teh Tarik theme

tokens · components · icons · motion

Shared logic core

draw schedules · entry & ledger state · 4D/TOTO analysis · notifications · navigation

Today, in this POC: each theme is its own standalone HTML file. At production: First launch → Theme Picker → choice persisted → app renders in that skin → changeable later in Preferences.

NumLah isn't shipped, and it isn't trying to predict anything. 4D and TOTO draws are independent random events with zero predictive signal — the product says so plainly, on every screen where it matters, rather than quietly implying otherwise.

What it demonstrates is a repeatable workflow: persona research → two theme prototypes over one shared logic → full bilingual support → a systematic, in-browser QA pass — carried out end-to-end through AI-assisted, vibe-coded development alongside Claude.

AI Feature Backlog

The AI features worth building — and the one that isn't.

Claude built this prototype. The more interesting question is which AI features belong inside the shipped product — and which would break it.

01

OCR ticket entry

Photograph a physical ticket instead of keying in numbers. Extends an existing flow rather than inventing a new one — the safest place to start.

02

Insight copy generation

Persona-tuned phrasing for history and Reality Check screens, in either theme voice and both languages. Descriptive framing only.

03

Responsible-play detection

The most distinctive feature here — and the one needing real product and clinical input on triggers and copy before it ships. Not something to improvise.

04

Number suggestion — never

No "AI picks your numbers," no "suggested numbers," not even played for laughs in Singlish. That's the line between tracking what you play and implying a pattern predicts a random draw.

Sequencing matters. OCR entry and insight generation extend what already exists — build those first. Responsible-play detection is the most valuable of the three, and precisely for that reason it doesn't get improvised: triggers and copy need clinical and product input, not a designer's best guess.

From build tool to product runtime. All three could run on the Claude API inside the shipped app, not just inside the build process. "Built with Claude" describes how a prototype got made. "Built with Claude, powered by Claude" describes a product where the same reasoning that shaped the design also runs in production — where AI isn't scaffolding, it's something users actually touch.

That shift also changes what the guardrails mean. The hard no on prediction stops being a principle written on a page and becomes an engineering boundary: a system prompt, a refusal path, a set of behaviours the model has to hold under real user pressure. Designing responsible AI in a spec is one thing. Shipping it is another.
