Case Study 03 · Capability Building & L&D

Teaching what actually works — a UX curriculum built from practice, not theory.

As Head of Academic Delivery at Skills Union, I led the design and delivery of the UX Design & Product Management (UXPM) programme — Design Thinking as its foundational framework, spanning UX design and product management disciplines — created and delivered under Singapore's SGUnited Skills (SGUS) Programme, the SkillsFuture Career Transition Programme (SCTP), a custom track for the Institute of Finance and Banking (IBF) and other short workshops in partnership with NTU.

RoleHead of Academic Delivery
MethodCurriculum design × instructor enablement × change management
Scope3 NTU-partnered programmes · 20+ instructors · 500+ graduates
StatusDelivered · Sep 2020 – Jul 2022
See the outcomes ↓ How the programme was built ↓

The Context

Effective capability building rarely comes from looking inward.

Drawing on my background in entrepreneurship and cross-industry UI/UX consultancy, I architected a curriculum designed for scale. It enabled a 20-person instructor team to deliver consistent, high-quality training that served as a direct career launchpad for our cohorts. The task was to harvest that range of experience and translate it into one scalable Learning & Development architecture, a set of knowledge and practice that is most relevant for the times (instead of pure theories) that allows graduates to be immediately useful and productive to any employers upon graduation.

What most L&D programmes optimise for

  • Off-the-shelf, theory-first curricula
  • Instructors as presenters, not facilitators
  • Training treated as an event, not a change process
  • One curriculum for every cohort, regardless of context
  • Success measured by completion, not outcome

What this programme asked instead

  • Can complex, multi-industry intelligence be distilled into a pragmatic, engaging curriculum?
  • Can a team of 20+ instructors deliver it with consistency and real-world credibility?
  • Can cohorts be mentored through genuine career and structural change, not just taught content?

These aren't just curriculum questions; they are questions of organisational design and change management applied to learning itself — the difference between a training course people sit through and a programme that measurably changes what people can do.

Delivering At Scale

From curriculum design to a placement outcome, one deliberate phase at a time.

Across the SGUnited Skills (SGUS), SkillsFuture Career Transition (SCTP) and IBF programmes — co-organised with NTU's PaCE@NTU — delivery moved through four connected phases.

01

Curriculum architecture

Cross-industry intelligence — from entrepreneurship to User Experience & User Interface Design consultancy — distilled into a pragmatic UX Design & Product Management curriculum, with Design Thinking as its foundational framework.

02

Instructor team build-out

20+ full-time and adjunct instructors recruited, aligned and managed under a single delivery standard across three concurrent programmes.

03

Cohort delivery & mentorship

Cohorts mentored through the material and through the career transition itself — treating learning as a change initiative, not a single event.

04

Outcome measurement

Completion, satisfaction and placement tracked as the real measure of success — not attendance.

Capability Demonstrated

What this programme proves.

Cross-industry translation

Genuinely disparate experience — entrepreneurship, UI/UX consultancy across diverse industries and client bases — distilled into one coherent, teachable curriculum.

Instructor & team leadership

20+ full-time and adjunct instructors aligned, managed and held to a consistent delivery standard across three concurrent programmes.

Change management applied to learning

Cohorts mentored through career transitions as a change initiative, not just taught content.

Outcome-driven design

Success measured by completion, NPS and six-month placement — not attendance.

Executive & stakeholder alignment

Delivered in partnership with NTU's PaCE@NTU, SSG and IBF, balancing academic rigor with employer-facing outcomes.

Where This Led

The same pattern, applied at greater scale.

2020–2022

Skills Union — Head of Academic Delivery. Built and led the instructor team and curriculum across three NTU-partnered programmes.

2022–2025

NEC Asia Pacific — Senior Innovation Consultant. Led the Experience Design workstream for Singapore's Energy Market Authority, translating the same cross-functional, change-management approach into public-sector transformation.

2025–2026

NTT DATA — User Experience Architect. Led the UX and Business Analysis workstream on a S$10M government transformation programme, and introduced AI-assisted delivery practices that cut turnaround time by 25%.

Now

Applying the same underlying discipline — leading and coaching teams towards best practice and quality outcomes — in AI-assisted enterprise transformation work today.

This programme wasn't a training course bolted onto a job title — it was proof that capability-building, done properly, is a design and change-management discipline in its own right.

What it demonstrates is a repeatable workflow: distill cross-industry intelligence → build and align an instructor team → mentor cohorts through real change → measure outcomes, not attendance — the same architecture now underpinning enterprise AI-transformation work.
